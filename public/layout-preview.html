Ten layouts, six kinds of motion

The same eight categories and the same photographs, laid out ten ways — then six motion treatments shown on their own, so layout and animation can be chosen separately. Everything is rendered on the site's own dark ground in the site's own typefaces.

Card copy is placeholder at two lengths, so you can judge how each layout handles short and long text. The category names are real.

A

Uniform grid

Live today

Eight equal tiles, the name burned into the photograph. Calm and even — no category outranks another.

DescriptionsCramped. Copy sits on the image and competes with it.

B

Caption block

Most legible

Photograph on top, name and copy on a solid panel beneath. Nothing overlaps the image, so text contrast is fixed instead of depending on how each photo happens to look.

DescriptionsBest. Copy has its own ground and can run to three lines.

C

Editorial bento

Closest to the reference sent

Two categories run double-width and carry copy; the other six stay small and name-only.

DescriptionsPartial — only the two feature tiles have room.

D

Portrait

Most fashion-led

Taller 3:4 crops with copy always visible. The vertical format flatters garment photography and gives the row a lookbook rhythm.

DescriptionsGood, if held to two lines.

E

Index rows

Densest

A two-column list — thumbnail, name, copy. Treats the range as a catalogue index rather than a picture grid, and scales past eight without the page growing tall.

DescriptionsBest for long copy. Rows take three or four lines.

Hover reveal

Quietest at rest

Only the name shows until the cursor arrives, then the copy rises into view. The grid stays uncluttered and the photography does the talking.

DescriptionsHidden until hover — see Motion 6 for the version that works on phones.

Type-led index

Most editorial

The names become the design — large serif set against hairline rules, with the photograph reduced to a small square. Reads like a fashion house's range page.

I

Duotone

Most industrial

The house green washed over each photograph, with technical uppercase labels. Ties the range visually to the rest of the brand rather than to the photos.

DescriptionsName only — deliberately terse.

J

Staggered

Most rhythmic

Alternating tall and short tiles. Same content as the uniform grid, but the uneven baseline stops the block feeling like a spreadsheet.

DescriptionsSame constraint as A — copy would sit on the photo.

Motion

The same four cards, animated six ways. Each treatment lists what happens on a desktop and what happens on a phone, because hover does not exist on touch — a layout that hides its copy behind hover simply hides it on mobile. Use the Phone button above to see the touch behaviour without leaving your laptop.

  • 1 Lift and zoom
  • 2 Curtain wipe
  • 3 Slow drift
  • 4 Focus
  • 5 Cascade
  • 6 Tap to expand
1

Lift and zoom

On the site today

The photograph scales gently and the card rises a few pixels.

DesktopPointer over a card.

PhoneNothing — hover does not exist. The card only fades in as you scroll to it.

2

Curtain wipe

Most deliberate

A green curtain sweeps up over the photograph and the name shifts with it.

DesktopPointer over a card.

PhoneThe same wipe plays automatically as each card scrolls into view.

3

Slow drift

Best on phones

The photograph pans and scales continuously on a long, slow loop — a Ken Burns move. No input needed, so a phone sees exactly what a laptop sees.

DesktopAlways running.

PhoneAlways running — identical.

4

Focus

Most dramatic

The card under attention stays bright while its neighbours dim and desaturate.

DesktopThe hovered card is focused, the rest recede.

PhoneThe card nearest the middle of the screen focuses itself as you scroll.

5

Cascade

Most cinematic on arrival

Cards rise and fade in sequence rather than all at once, so the row assembles itself.

DesktopPlays once as the row enters view.

PhoneIdentical — it is driven by scroll, not pointer.

6

Tap to expand

Fixes the mobile gap

The description is hidden until asked for. This is direction F's motion, but with a touch equivalent so the copy is reachable on a phone.

DesktopCopy slides up on hover.

PhoneTap a card to open its copy; tap again to close.

Choosing

Two answers, not one: a letter for the layout and a number for the motion. They combine freely — layout B with motion 3, for instance.

If the range has to read the same on a phone as on a laptop, motion 3 or 5 are the safe picks — neither needs a pointer. Motion 6 is the one to choose if the descriptions should stay hidden until asked for.

Reply with a letter A–J and a number 1–6. Anyone with reduced motion turned on sees every card static — that is deliberate and built in.
